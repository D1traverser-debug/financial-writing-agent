# Hooks & Retention — Earn the Next Paragraph

Status: `CURRENT_CANDIDATE_1.0`
Owner: `DOMAIN:financial_writing`

## Purpose

Hook quality is not a headline trick. The product must earn a sequence of reader decisions from click/open through completion. Title, opening, H1 transitions and ending are one retention system.

## Reader-retention model

At every important transition ask:
1. What concrete information has the reader received?
2. What material question remains open?
3. Why is the next paragraph/section likely to answer something the reader now cares about?
4. Are we adding new information, or merely restating the thesis in different words?

A reader gap is useful only when it is concrete, relevant and paid off. Empty suspense or withholding basic orientation is clickbait, not retention design.

## Title

A title should expose enough hard information and stake for a cold finance reader to care while leaving a real unresolved payoff.

Useful angle families include:
- hard fact/anomaly;
- company divergence;
- profit/order transmission;
- policy transmission;
- supply/demand conflict;
- expectation gap;
- global comparison;
- validation/risk;
- market-selection question.

For material long-form work, compare genuinely different title/angle strategies rather than synonym variants. Existing headline-package candidate diversity rules remain useful for preventing first-answer convergence.

Reject:
- vague “重磅/大机会/深度解析” language without information;
- titles that answer nothing;
- titles that fully exhaust the article so there is no further payoff;
- misleading certainty;
- curiosity wording disconnected from the evidence.

## Opening

The opening needs a concrete anchor: number, company conflict, rule change, case, scene or observable contradiction.

It should quickly establish, in whatever order fits the material:
- what changed / what is strange;
- why it matters financially;
- what question the article will resolve;
- enough early payoff to prove the article has substance;
- a remaining payoff worth continuing for.

There is no mandatory sentence-1/sentence-2/sentence-3 syntax. The old “first three sentences” idea is retained only as a cold-reader diagnostic zone, not a prose formula.

Do not open with generic context such as “近年来……快速发展”“随着……不断深入”“值得关注的是……”. If background is necessary, earn it after a concrete reader reason exists.

## Section-level retention

Every H1 needs:
- an information-bearing numbered heading;
- a reader question;
- new evidence/argument;
- a company/case/number/contradiction when it materially clarifies the mechanism;
- a section payoff;
- a bridge or unresolved consequence that makes the next section useful.

A section that only says “接下来我们看……” or “值得关注的还有……” without a new information gap is weak navigation.

## Completion / payoff

The ending must resolve the promise made by the title/opening. It can deliver:
- a corrected mental model;
- a company/value-chain distinction;
- a causal map;
- a set of verification/change conditions;
- a bounded answer to the central question.

Do not replace payoff with generic monitoring language or an unearned investment implication.

## Reader Retention Audit

The current retention pass must identify concrete drop-off points using text evidence. It should cite locations/excerpts for:
- slow or generic opening;
- repeated conclusions;
- sections with low information gain;
- detached technical exposition;
- company examples appearing too late;
- broken transitions;
- title/opening promise not paid off;
- ending that merely summarizes.

The critic must not defend the writer's intent. “The article intended to…” is not evidence that a cold reader experiences the promised value.

## Metrics and live learning

Clicks, dwell/finish behavior, likes, saves, comments, user edits and explicit rejection are all evidence with different meanings. A high click rate alone does not prove reader satisfaction or completion. Post-publication/user outcome evidence should feed the evolution corpus with platform/context attached rather than being generalized blindly.
