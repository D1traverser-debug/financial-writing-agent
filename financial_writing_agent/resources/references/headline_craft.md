# Headline & Hook Craft Pack

## Purpose
This is a technique pack subordinate to the Hook Engine in `references/editorial.md`. It does not replace evidence, acceptance contracts or platform rules. Its job is to improve headline generation and selection without collapsing into clickbait formulas.

The target is **high information scent + one valuable unresolved hinge + reliable payoff**.

A strong headline should let the reader recognize the subject and why it matters, while leaving one specific thing worth opening the article to resolve.

## The headline promise equation
A useful finance headline normally combines four jobs:

`recognizable anchor + reader stake/tension + one information gap + credible payoff signal`

- **Recognizable anchor**: the concrete topic/company/policy/number/industry must be identifiable quickly.
- **Stake/tension**: why the reader should care now — profit transmission, cost, valuation, orders, competitive position, market selection, risk, timing, or another supported consequence.
- **One information gap**: the title withholds one useful answer, not the whole subject.
- **Credible payoff signal**: enough specificity that the reader can predict what kind of answer the article will deliver.

Do not maximize all four mechanically. The art is choosing the smallest combination that creates a clean click reason.

## Specificity sweet spot
Do not confuse curiosity with vagueness.

Too vague:
- topic identity unclear;
- pronouns/“这件事/这个信号” without a useful referent;
- promises surprise but gives no stake;
- requires a click merely to understand what the article is about.

Too complete:
- gives the full thesis, ranking and causal answer in the title;
- leaves no meaningful reason to open;
- becomes a mini abstract rather than a headline.

Aim for the middle: **tell the reader enough to form a salient question, not enough to close it.**

## Build the title from evidence, not from wording tricks
Before writing candidates, extract a compact `HeadlineRawMaterial` set:
1. safest high-value factual anchor;
2. strongest consequence/stake;
3. strongest contradiction or expectation mismatch;
4. strongest company divergence / `why_this vs why_not`;
5. strongest number/scale/timing anchor;
6. one question the article genuinely resolves;
7. the first-screen fact/mechanism that can pay the click immediately.

If the raw material is weak, better wording will not rescue the title. Fix the thesis/research first.

## Candidate-generation method
Generate 8–12 candidates across **different reasoning angles**, not just different syntax.

Useful angle families when evidence supports them:
- **Paradox / anti-expectation**: a verified outcome runs against the default intuition.
- **Hidden bottleneck**: attention is on A, but the economic constraint sits at B.
- **Consequence / money path**: a small event changes cash flow, margin, orders, inventory or valuation logic.
- **Company divergence**: same theme, materially different realization stages or market roles.
- **Selection question**: why the market/company ranking differs from industrial authenticity.
- **Number / scale**: a verified number changes the reader's mental model.
- **Timing / inflection**: something materially changed now, not merely “is important.”
- **Causal why**: a non-obvious mechanism explains an observed result.
- **Result-first**: state the surprising result, leave the mechanism to the article.
- **Finance-wide translation**: convert a niche sector story into a general money/competition/cash-flow question.

Do not force every article to use every family.

## Controlled withholding
A headline may withhold the **answer**, but should not withhold the **subject** or the **reason to care**.

Good withholding:
- “AI把筛药速度拉满，实验室怎么反而更忙了？” — subject and paradox are clear; mechanism remains open.
- “一个3欧元关税，为什么把跨境电商从抢流量推向抢现金流？” — event and consequence are clear; causal chain remains open.

Bad withholding:
- “一个被所有人忽略的信号，正在改变一切”;
- “这家公司真正的秘密，终于藏不住了”;
- “答案可能和你想的不一样”.

The reader should never need to click merely to discover the noun.

## Question-headline discipline
Use a question title only when:
- the question is not trivial;
- the article actually resolves it;
- the first screen begins paying it immediately;
- the title does not hide a known answer solely to manufacture suspense.

A weak question headline often has an obvious answer, a yes/no answer, or a payoff that arrives hundreds of words later.

## Headline trust boundary
Do not create a false inference that the body later walks back.

Reject a headline when the most natural reader interpretation is materially stronger than the evidence, even if a lawyer could defend the literal wording.

Examples of trust failures:
- “爆发” when only one quarter improved;
- “订单暴增” when the number includes a one-off transfer/milestone;
- “最大受益者” without a defined comparison set;
- “AI带来利润翻倍” when AI contribution is not separately disclosed;
- “政策落地” when the item remains draft/guidance/expectation.

A title is part of the factual product, not a marketing exemption.

## Information scent and scanability
The reader often sees the title out of context.

Prefer:
- recognizable nouns early;
- the decisive company/event/number before ornamental language;
- common words over clever but obscure synonyms;
- one main cognitive job per headline.

Avoid stacking too many entities, clauses and technical terms. A sophisticated thesis may need a simpler headline and a richer lead.

## AI-headline fingerprint audit
The following forms are not banned, but repeated use creates a synthetic house smell:
- “为什么X正在重塑Y？”;
- “真正的X，不是A，而是B”;
- “被低估的X”;
- “一个X，为什么把Y从A推向B？”;
- “当X遇上Y，谁会受益？”;
- “一文看懂…”;
- “X背后的投资机会”;
- “最值得关注的N家公司”.

If recent outputs repeatedly use the same grammatical skeleton, force candidate generation from different angles before selection.

## Title tournament: destructive selection
Do not select the title because it “sounds strongest.” Eliminate candidates through these tests.

### Hard-fail tests
1. **Accuracy** — is every material word supported?
2. **Natural interpretation** — would an ordinary reader infer something stronger than the article proves?
3. **Subject clarity** — can the topic be identified without clicking?
4. **Payoff** — does the first screen begin answering the promised gap?
5. **Uniqueness** — could the same title be pasted onto another sector by changing two nouns?
6. **No backstage leak** — no source/tool/report-production language.

Any hard fail eliminates the candidate.

### Comparative tests
Rank survivors by:
- click reason strength;
- information scent;
- financial stake;
- novelty of angle relative to recent outputs;
- cognitive load;
- company/stock concreteness when relevant;
- trust / long-term account value;
- how naturally the lead continues from the headline.

The winning title is the best **promise**, not the loudest sentence.

## Headline → lead → H1 contract
The title should open one main loop.

The first 1–3 sentences should:
1. confirm that the title was not bait;
2. deliver one concrete fact/image/consequence;
3. sharpen the unresolved hinge rather than restarting from background.

The first H1 should then deepen the same question instead of switching to a generic “industry background” section.

If the lead could be attached to several different candidate titles unchanged, the title/lead contract is probably weak.

## FINANCE_DEEP vs FINANCE_BROAD
### FINANCE_DEEP title
Prioritize:
- precision;
- analytical contradiction;
- company/industry mechanism;
- the reason the thesis matters to a serious finance reader.

It may carry slightly more information when the audience tolerates it.

### FINANCE_BROAD title
Prioritize:
- instant recognizability;
- low jargon;
- one vivid contradiction/consequence;
- a clear finance-wide stake;
- a fast first-screen payoff.

Broad may simplify the packaging, not the truth.

## Headline learning discipline
Post-publication performance can inform future candidate generation only as structured experience.

Do not infer “question titles work”, “numbers work”, “negative titles work” or similar laws from one article. Preserve title, platform, topic, timing, impressions/clicks/read depth when available, and confounders. Repeated comparable evidence may support a platform-specific hypothesis; it does not automatically become a universal writing rule.

## Final rule
A good headline does not hide the article. It **compresses the reason to read it**.
