# Human Voice Craft Pack

## Purpose
This is a technique pack subordinate to `references/style.md`. It does not replace evidence, editorial or compliance rules. Its job is to turn accurate finance analysis into prose that feels authored, observed and alive rather than assembled by a language model.

The target is not “write like a novel.” The target is **human explanatory finance**: factual like research, readable like strong narrative nonfiction, paced with some of the craft of fiction and essays, but never allowed to invent a scene, thought, quote, emotion or fact.

## What to borrow from other genres
### From narrative nonfiction
Borrow:
- verified concrete scenes/actions/objects as doorways;
- selective telling detail;
- camera distance and pacing;
- one central story question/tension;
- movement between concrete detail and abstract meaning;
- understatement: let a strong fact carry its own drama.

Do not borrow:
- reconstructed scenes that the evidence cannot support;
- invented dialogue;
- invented inner thoughts;
- cinematic detail added only because it sounds vivid.

### From fiction craft
Borrow only the mechanics that survive a truth constraint:
- conflict/friction;
- cause → consequence;
- setup → complication → payoff;
- contrast between actors with different incentives;
- rhythm and controlled withholding;
- a detail that changes how the reader sees the situation.

Do not turn companies into cartoon characters, force a hero/villain arc, or manufacture suspense after the answer is already known.

### From essays / literary prose
Borrow:
- a recognizable author stance;
- sentence music and paragraph rhythm;
- observation that notices what is odd, costly, ironic or revealing;
- a line that can carry thought without becoming a slogan.

Do not borrow decorative lyricism, ornate metaphor or self-display that slows the financial argument.

## The five human-reading engines
Adapted from general narrative/retention practice for finance.

1. **Promise** — what useful question does the reader expect this passage to resolve?
2. **Tension** — what contradiction, trade-off, uncertainty or competing explanation keeps the next paragraph necessary?
3. **Scene life** — can at least part of the mechanism be shown through a verified action, object, workflow, transaction, number or consequence rather than explained only as abstraction?
4. **Language experience** — do sentence length, paragraph size and emphasis change with the material, or does everything have the same machine-polished cadence?
5. **Authored judgment** — does the writer select, compare and prioritize, or merely summarize all sides into safe neutrality?

A finance article can be logically correct and still read dead when several of these engines are near zero.

## Concrete ↔ abstract movement
Use a ladder of abstraction rather than living in the middle rung of jargon.

Useful motion:
`verified concrete detail/action → mechanism → financial meaning → company consequence → concrete number/company again`

Examples of low-rung material:
- one production bottleneck;
- one contract term;
- one order/backlog number;
- one laboratory step;
- one price change;
- one balance-sheet item;
- one market-day divergence.

The concrete detail must be material. Do not add sensory decoration that changes nothing.

## Real friction creates human texture
Low-quality generated prose is often unnaturally clean: every source answers the question, every causal link works, every company fits the thesis, every paragraph lands on cue.

Real analytical prose should retain material friction when it exists:
- the metric cannot be compared cleanly;
- an order may not become revenue;
- one company has the better story but weaker earnings realization;
- AI increases project volume but may reduce labor hours per project;
- a policy helps demand but compresses price;
- one data point contradicts the neat thesis;
- the market trades the opposite of the fundamental ranking.

Do not manufacture mess. Preserve **real** mess instead of editing it away for rhetorical smoothness.

## Show once, explain once
When a fact/action already demonstrates the point, do not immediately restate the same conclusion in abstract language.

Bad pattern:
`concrete example → abstract explanation of the same example → summary sentence repeating both`

Prefer:
`concrete example → one inference that advances the thesis`

If the reader can already infer the point, trust them.

## Sentence and paragraph rhythm
Human rhythm is responsive, not randomized.

- Use short sentences for impact, reversal or a hard judgment — not as a permanent house style.
- Let difficult mechanisms breathe across longer sentences when needed.
- Avoid repeating the same paragraph pattern across sections: setup sentence → three facts → “这意味着” conclusion.
- Do not make every paragraph similar in length.
- Do not force fragments, slang or grammatical roughness to “look human.”
- A long analytical sentence followed by one short landing sentence can be effective when the material earns it.

## Humor
Humor is optional and subordinate to understanding.

Useful humor:
- exposes a mechanism or absurdity;
- releases cognitive pressure after dense explanation;
- comes from the situation rather than a joke template;
- remains dry/light rather than clownish.

Reject humor that:
- mocks losses or retail investors;
- trivializes compliance/risk;
- replaces evidence;
- repeats as a gimmick every few paragraphs;
- makes the author more visible than the analysis.

## Voice without fake intimacy
A finance writer may sound like a person without constantly saying “我们”“你看”“朋友们”“说人话”.

Voice comes from:
- what the writer notices;
- which comparison is chosen;
- where skepticism appears;
- what gets compressed or expanded;
- sentence rhythm;
- the willingness to make an evidence-bounded judgment.

Do not confuse directness with teacher-talk or influencer intimacy.

## AI-tone failure patterns
Treat these as pattern alarms, not forbidden words:
- repeated `不是X，而是Y` or `真正/核心/本质上` pivots;
- every H1 starts by rephrasing its heading;
- every company paragraph uses identical slots: “业务—数据—优势—风险—结论”;
- the writer explains what the reader has already understood;
- every transition is explicit logic glue: “因此/所以/这意味着/值得注意的是”;
- all uncertainty is neatly converted into a three-item list;
- every paragraph ends in a polished abstract sentence;
- metaphors arrive on schedule rather than from the subject;
- the prose never encounters a dead end, counterexample, ambiguity or awkward trade-off;
- the article has no memorable concrete detail despite thousands of words.

## Fresh-reader blind pass for finance
Before release, run one pass that sees only the reader-visible final article — not TopicDNA, research notes, acceptance contracts or writer explanations.

It must answer:
1. Where did the article first genuinely catch attention?
2. Which three locations are the likeliest exit points, and why?
3. What does the reader think the article has promised by the end of the first screen?
4. Which unknowns are productive curiosity, and which are simply unclear writing?
5. Where does the prose feel like a checklist/report converted into paragraphs?
6. Which rhetorical device or sentence shape is repeated enough to become visible?
7. Which paragraph sounds most like a generic AI summary and should be rewritten or cut?
8. What one concrete detail/number/comparison is most memorable after reading?
9. Does the author sound like a real analyst with a point of view, or like a neutral synthesis engine?
10. If all headings were removed, would the argument still feel like one continuous act of reasoning?

A serious failure here requires revision even when factual and mechanical checks pass.

## Final rule
Do not try to “add human flavor” after drafting by sprinkling jokes, metaphors or fragments. Human texture must come from the **way the argument is observed, selected, sequenced and voiced**.
