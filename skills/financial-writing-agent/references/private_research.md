# Private Research Brief — Mandatory Pre-Publication Research Layer

Status: `CURRENT_CANDIDATE_1.1`
Owner: `DOMAIN:financial_writing`

## Purpose

Every new Financial Writing task must carry a durable `PrivateResearchBrief` through Research before publication planning. The private brief is the full analytical workbench; the article is a selective reader product derived from it.

The brief exists to prevent four recurring failures:
1. a publishable narrative being chosen before the full company/value-chain/market structure is understood;
2. material event or policy catalysts being omitted because research starts from the currently fashionable narrative;
3. within-segment winners being identified without a second, cross-segment market-selection pass;
4. private decision-useful research being lost because it would be too detailed, tactical or process-heavy for publication copy.

The brief is not itself a buy/sell instruction. Existing Financial redlines on entry timing, target price, position sizing and covert equivalents remain in force.

## Version compatibility

New tasks use contract `1.1`. Historical tasks explicitly carrying `1.0` remain valid against the original 1.0 module set and are not retroactively rewritten. A new 1.1 task may not submit a 1.0 brief to bypass the added research jobs.

## Required modules

Every brief contains every module required by its contract version. A module is `APPLICABLE` or `NOT_MATERIAL`. `NOT_MATERIAL` requires an explicit reason; it is not a shortcut for omitted research. An applicable module requires evidence refs.

### 1. `company_universe_and_value_chain`

Build the broadest evidence-bounded listed-company universe relevant to the theme/event before selecting favorites. Separate, when material:
- economic-substance universe;
- market/concept-label universe;
- included / excluded / unresolved names;
- value-chain class for every retained material company;
- direct, first-order, second-order, weak and negative exposure.

Do not claim literal internet exhaustiveness. Use coverage state plus auditable saturation/gaps.

### 2. `business_granularity`

For each material company capture the decision-relevant business facts available for the topic, such as:
- revenue/profit or segment contribution;
- product/service and monetization model;
- customers, channels and geography;
- orders, shipments, paid members, transaction volume or other operating realization;
- business stage: scaled, operating/expanding, small-scale realized, trial/expansion, plan/agreement, or label-only;
- benefit mechanism and reversal/adverse variables.

High overseas revenue is not automatically high thematic purity. Export manufacturing, direct cross-border retail, B2B services, logistics, payment and marketing must not be collapsed into one bucket.

### 3. `market_cap_and_liquidity`

When listed-equity market behavior is material, timestamp and record for every short-horizon/material candidate:
- total market capitalization;
- float market capitalization;
- turnover rate;
- traded value/liquidity;
- board regime where relevant.

Private deliverables visually emphasize total and float market cap. Market cap is a market-selection variable, not evidence of business quality.

### 4. `catalyst_strength_and_freshness`

Classify each material catalyst before mapping stocks:
- sudden vs expected;
- official/primary vs rumor/secondary;
- new information vs stale/recycled information;
- expectation vs implementation vs financial realization;
- strength/coverage: systemic multi-link catalyst, focused industry catalyst, verification catalyst, or weak label/old-news catalyst.

A stronger and broader catalyst should trigger research into a wider benefit chain and possible market-role ladder; do not assume every catalyst can support a full theme.

### 5. `benefit_chain_tiering`

Map the catalyst through economics before ranking securities:
`trigger/change -> mechanism -> exposure -> operating/financial expression -> verification/reversal`.

Distinguish:
- directly helped / directly hurt;
- first-order beneficiaries;
- second-order beneficiaries;
- weak/optional exposure;
- companies whose business is real but whose profit transmission is not established.

### 6. `short_horizon_role_map`

When short-horizon A-share behavior is material, assign roles as hypotheses supported by evidence, not labels pasted after the fact:
- pioneer / first price discovery;
- sentiment high / space leader;
- theme leader;
- capacity middle-army / trend leader;
- branch leader;
- assist;
- card-position competitor;
- catch-up;
- follower / weak tag;
- eliminated / failed candidate;
- existing-height theme remapping candidate.

Role semantics:
- a high board is not automatically the theme leader;
- a pioneer may be replaced later;
- a middle-army validates capacity and industry participation rather than board height;
- card-position requires a real competing role/height and observable displacement;
- catch-up normally depends on height/attention already created elsewhere;
- roles are not quotas: one company may legitimately combine roles, and a market with insufficient breadth/liquidity may support only one true core.

Keep `business strongest` separate from `most likely to become a short-horizon limit-up/sentiment core`.

### 7. `market_structure_and_positioning`

When market behavior is material, timestamp the relevant environment:
- sentiment cycle: launch, expansion, divergence, repair, retreat;
- current maximum board height and important height tiers;
- 1->2, 2->3 or other promotion/attrition where available;
- limit-up, broken-board, strong-stock and prior-limit-up pools when available;
- recent stock character: limit-up count, maximum consecutive limits, reason/driver, failed boards, reversal, A-kill or rebound memory;
- market/theme liquidity and whether current turnover can support one core or a multi-branch ladder;
- whether the theme has isolated boards, branch breadth, a leader, a capacity confirmation and a credible ladder.

Use market structure to explain selection pressure; do not infer business truth from price action.

### 8. `counterfactual_company_comparison`

For every serious candidate set, answer:
**Why would the market/economics select A rather than B under this exact catalyst?**

First compare within each relevant segment. Compare topic-specific variables such as business purity, realization, scarcity, catalyst fit, market cap, board regime, liquidity, current height, recent stock character, competing themes and negative evidence.

A company list without this counterfactual comparison is not a finished private research brief.

### 9. `negative_and_falsification_pool`

Preserve companies/facts that weaken the preferred story:
- false/weak thematic labels;
- old news repeatedly recycled;
- zero-revenue/planning-only exposure;
- current price move driven by another theme;
- business growth without profit realization;
- high-position or failed-promotion risk;
- policy direction that helps one branch while hurting another.

Rejected or failed candidates remain evidence; do not delete them after a winner is selected.

### 10. `scenario_tree`

Build scenario-dependent role maps before the event happens. Different catalysts should produce different expected winners.

For a broad, high-strength catalyst, test whether a plausible ladder can form:
`pioneer -> theme/space leader -> capacity middle-army -> branch leaders -> assists -> low-position catch-up -> elimination`.

For a narrow catalyst, explicitly resist inventing a broad ladder when only one branch has causal exposure.

### 11. `next_window_validation`

State what observable next-session/next-catalyst evidence would:
- confirm theme formation;
- confirm a leader or branch leader;
- prove real card-position displacement;
- confirm capacity participation;
- distinguish an isolated stock move from sector formation;
- falsify the preferred thesis or role map.

This is a verification plan, not an entry-price instruction.

### 12. `publication_distillation`

The private brief and the published article have different jobs.

Record explicitly:
- the private conclusions that must inform `FINANCE_DEEP`;
- the narrower acquisition/payoff model for `FINANCE_BROAD`;
- material research that should stay private because it is process-heavy, tactical or distracting;
- which company comparisons/causal chains are essential to the article;
- which cross-segment comparison is needed for the reader to understand why one business model or company has the stronger logic;
- which internal vocabulary (for example exhaustive-universe bookkeeping, scoring tables, ladder mechanics) should not be narrated unless it improves the reader product.

For Deep, material multi-company sections should prefer causal comparison over parallel company profiles: explain why A has stronger/weaker transmission, durability or realization than B under the same event.

A publication may be selective. The research brief may not silently become selective just because the article is.

## Contract 1.1 additional mandatory modules

### 13. `material_catalyst_and_policy_ledger`

Before choosing the headline catalyst, build an evidence-bounded ledger of **all known materially relevant events under the declared topic/time scope**. Do not limit the ledger to events that support the preferred thesis.

For each material event record, as available:
- event/policy name and jurisdiction;
- announcement date, effective date and known expiry/review date;
- official/primary source and current status;
- whether it is proposal, announcement, implementation, suspension, extension, rollback, retaliation, exemption or financial realization;
- direction: positive, negative, mixed or branch-specific;
- what changed versus the previous regime;
- affected value-chain branches and likely first/second-order transmission;
- whether the market has already traded it and what genuinely new information remains;
- reversal/renewal/negotiation conditions.

**Policy research must be chronological and two-sided.** If a policy tightened and was later suspended, reduced, extended, exempted, retaliated against or replaced, retain the whole material sequence. A later easing cannot disappear because the current article is framed around tightening, and vice versa. Tariff work, for example, must check reciprocal/counter-tariff changes, de minimis rules, exemptions, suspensions, negotiated reductions and extensions when they materially affect the theme.

`COMPLETE` is illegal if a known material policy/event phase within scope is omitted. Noise and immaterial headlines need not be listed, but the exclusion boundary must be explainable.

### 14. `driver_sustainability_operability_and_concept_map`

For every serious thesis/candidate, distinguish four questions instead of collapsing them into one score:

**Driver**
- What is the immediate driver: policy, order, price, product, capacity, earnings, M&A, platform rule, supply shock, demand shock, event/meeting, media narrative or market sentiment?
- Is the driver company-specific, branch-specific, theme-wide or market-wide?

**Sustainability**
- What keeps the logic alive after the first reaction: recurring earnings, continuing policy implementation, order visibility, capacity cycle, repeated data verification, scarce supply, continuing narrative or only short-lived sentiment?
- What date/event naturally decays or refreshes the catalyst?

**Market operability**
- Without giving entry instructions, assess whether the thesis is realistically expressible in the current A-share market: board regime, total/float cap, liquidity, turnover, current height, recent stock character, crowding, competing themes, expected capacity role and whether the market can fund the name/theme.
- Separate “economically right but hard for short-horizon price discovery” from “economically weaker but highly recognizable/operable”.

**Concept/sector map**
- Record the stock's primary industry, material concept labels and event-relevant overlap/stacking themes.
- Identify which label is the current price driver and which labels are merely optional mappings.
- Track competing concept identities that may strengthen or contaminate attribution.

The brief should make clear whether sustainability and market operability point to the same company. They often do not.

### 15. `cross_segment_global_selection`

After every relevant segment has produced its own comparison/winner set, run a **second-stage all-domain comparison** across branch winners, sentiment/high-position candidates and capacity candidates.

This pass answers:
**Given this exact catalyst, current market liquidity and current sentiment structure, if capital can select only one or a few names, which role(s) are most likely to survive and why?**

Required considerations, when material:
- catalyst directness and benefit magnitude;
- financial realization and durability;
- scarcity / uniqueness;
- business-stage evidence;
- total/float market cap, liquidity and board regime;
- current height, recent stock character and first-price-discovery advantage;
- concept recognition and competing-theme contamination;
- current total market/theme turnover and how many cores the market can realistically support;
- whether the economic leader and sentiment leader are the same company;
- strongest challenger(s) and explicit role-switch conditions.

Do not force one leader, one middle-army and one branch leader merely because the vocabulary exists. Role cardinality follows evidence. Under constrained liquidity there may be only one dominant core; under broad systemic catalysts there may be one theme leader plus one capacity core and genuine branch leaders. A single stock may combine high-position, theme-leader and capacity roles if the observed market supports that interpretation.

The module must end with an evidence-bounded **global selection conclusion** rather than a collection of unrelated segment champions. If no unique core is justified, say so and preserve the unresolved competition plus the next-window conditions that would resolve it.

## Coverage and freshness

`coverage_state` is `COMPLETE` only when no known material research gap remains under the declared scope. `BOUNDED_WITH_GAPS` requires explicit unresolved gaps.

Time-sensitive fields — market cap, float cap, turnover, board height, promotion rates, policy state, orders, current business data and material catalyst status — must carry an `as_of` boundary and be refreshed when material to the next decision.

For 1.1 tasks, coverage also requires the event/policy ledger to reach evidence-bounded saturation and the global-selection pass to consume the material segment winners. A within-segment comparison alone is not terminal research.

## Source/tool priority

Prefer free or already-available authoritative sources/tools when they can supply the required evidence reliably. For policy/events, prefer primary government/regulator/exchange/company sources before secondary summaries. Use paid/external crawling only when it materially improves coverage or a needed source cannot be obtained through the free/primary path. Tool cost never justifies inventing missing evidence.

## Admission boundary

For marker-enabled new tasks, `financial_writing_agent.current_admission` persists the validated brief in `quality_evidence.private_research_brief` and binds its digest in the RESEARCH execution receipt. A side file or chat statement is not equivalent.

Revision back to RESEARCH invalidates the admitted brief together with downstream research quality evidence. A materially changed catalyst, company universe, market structure, policy phase or new counterexample requires the affected private-research modules to be refreshed before re-admission.
