# Market explanation completeness module

## Objective
When a finance article materially discusses A-share companies, themes, price reactions, limit-ups, leadership or continuation, research must explain **why the market selected some names before or more strongly than others** without turning the article into buy/sell guidance.

This is not a fixed “catalyst → concept → stock → funds → continuation” template. Treat that sequence as one useful hypothesis family. The governing method is **multi-hypothesis market explanation**: map the economically relevant universe, identify competing mechanisms, compare companies cross-sectionally, grade evidence strength and retain unresolved explanations when the data do not justify certainty.

## 1. Separate two questions that are often wrongly merged
### Economic-benefit question
Who is actually exposed to the event, through what business/industry path, with what expected timing and magnitude of financial realization?

### Market-selection question
Why did price discovery / attention / order flow select this stock, this subtheme or this market role at this moment rather than another economically related name?

A company can be a strong industrial beneficiary but a weak short-term market leader. A stock can be the attention or liquidity leader while having weaker direct earnings exposure. The article must not collapse these roles.

## 2. Explanation layers to investigate
For stock/theme-related topics, investigate the layers below when material. They are dimensions, not a mandatory article outline.

### A. Catalyst novelty and expectation gap
- What objectively changed, and when?
- Was the information genuinely new, a confirmation, a surprise relative to expectations, or merely newly salient?
- Is the market reacting to the fact itself, to a change in probability, to timing, or to the size of the expected economic effect?

### B. Concept / subtheme / economic-link map
- Enumerate the materially relevant concepts and subthemes, then the A-share companies linked to each.
- Distinguish direct business exposure, customer/supplier linkage, second-order exposure, capacity/price sensitivity, optionality and narrative-only association.
- Do not equate a vendor concept label or media association with verified economic exposure.

### C. Fundamental realization and sensitivity
For each material company/class, ask what converts the event into financial consequences: price, volume, orders, capacity utilization, cost, revenue, margin, cash flow, capex, asset value, customer mix or another topic-specific channel.

Compare **sensitivity**, not just relevance. Material dimensions may include exposure purity, revenue/profit share, earnings elasticity, capacity headroom, order visibility, customer verification, balance-sheet constraints, valuation/expectation gap and realization timing.

### D. Cross-sectional market selection / price formation
When explaining “why this one moved first/more”, test multiple mechanisms instead of inventing a single story:
- information diffusion and lead-lag across economically linked firms or industries;
- investor attention / co-attention and narrative salience;
- order imbalance, liquidity, free float and price impact;
- prior positioning, crowding, expectation gap and available seller supply;
- market role (fundamental beneficiary, sentiment leader, liquidity carrier, follower/position stock, etc.);
- price-limit mechanics, ranking salience and herding when relevant;
- market-cap / tradability / institutional-vs-retail participation differences;
- other event-specific mechanisms supported by evidence.

These mechanisms can coexist and can point in opposite directions. Market price action itself is evidence that a selection occurred; it is **not** proof of why it occurred.

### E. Continuation versus exhaustion / reversal
Never infer persistence from a limit-up or strong move alone. Ask what would support continued price discovery and what would instead support temporary attention/price pressure.

Potential persistence evidence may include repeated fundamental confirmation, continued information diffusion, fresh order/customer evidence, broadening but orderly theme participation, persistent informed/institutional participation, limited supply response, or another topic-specific mechanism.

Potential exhaustion/reversal evidence may include attention-only buying pressure, crowding, weak fundamental realization, widening narrative/fundamental gap, one-off order imbalance, liquidity distortion, sharp retail attention spikes, failed follow-through or contradictory company evidence.

The article explains these conditions; it does not convert them into execution instructions.

## 3. Causal-confidence ladder
Every material explanation of market selection must fit one of four epistemic levels:

1. `VERIFIED_FACT` — directly evidenced fact or relationship relevant to the claim.
2. `SUPPORTED_MECHANISM` — mechanism supported by credible research plus task-specific evidence, but not proven as the unique cause of the observed move.
3. `PLAUSIBLE_INTERPRETATION` — reasonable market interpretation with some supporting signals but insufficient identification for a causal claim.
4. `UNRESOLVED` — competing explanations remain and evidence is insufficient.

Writing strength must match this level. Do not turn `PLAUSIBLE_INTERPRETATION` into “所以它涨停就是因为……”.

## 4. Required cross-sectional questions
For a stock-related FINANCE_DEEP article, the bounded research universe should be able to answer, where material:
- Which companies/classes could benefit at all?
- Which are direct vs indirect / narrative-only?
- Through what economic path can the benefit reach revenue/profit/cash flow, and on what horizon?
- Which names have higher or lower sensitivity, and why?
- Why might market attention/price discovery choose one name earlier or more strongly than peers?
- What evidence argues against that explanation?
- What conditions could sustain the move, and what conditions could imply exhaustion/reversal?
- Which important cases remain unexplained?

This is the meaning of **market-explanation completeness**. It is not a requirement to assert a confident answer for every stock.

## 5. FINANCE_DEEP expression contract
When this module activates, FINANCE_DEEP must cover every material explanation branch that could change the reader’s understanding of:
- who truly benefits;
- why benefits differ across companies;
- why market selection differs from fundamental benefit;
- why observed price leadership may persist or reverse;
- what evidence would falsify the current interpretation.

The article may group low-materiality names/classes, but it must not silently omit a material competing explanation just because one narrative reads better.

## 6. FINANCE_BROAD expression contract
FINANCE_BROAD can compress this map to the strongest 1–3 information gaps, but any retained market explanation must preserve its evidence grade and key counterevidence. Traffic optimization never licenses hindsight storytelling.

## 7. Compliance boundary
This module explains price formation and market selection; it does not provide actionable trading instructions, timing, target prices, position sizing, stop levels or “buy/sell/low-buy/chase-limit” guidance.

## 8. Durable research basis
The method is informed by established and recent evidence on limited attention, economically linked firms, industry information diffusion, order imbalance/liquidity, A-share attention/co-attention, price-limit salience/herding and reversal. These are **priors, not case verdicts**. Each article must verify current/task-specific facts and may reach a different explanation.

Useful durable anchors include work on economic links and delayed information incorporation; industry lead-lag and momentum; order imbalance and price formation; investor attention and attention-grabbing stocks; A-share co-attention; and Chinese price-limit/limit-hit behavior. New evidence should update this module when it materially changes the explanatory framework.
