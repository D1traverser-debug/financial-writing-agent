# Market Explanation Completeness module

## Ownership and activation
This module owns **cross-sectional market-selection / price-formation explanation**. It activates only when `ResearchPackage.market_explanation_scope = REQUIRED`.

A company universe, industry comparison or earnings-beneficiary list does **not** activate this module by itself. Use `NOT_MATERIAL` when the article can answer its financial question through economic exposure/fundamentals without claiming why one stock was selected earlier or more strongly by the market.

When activated, the objective is to explain observed price selection without turning a plausible story into proven causality or executable trading advice.

## Separate two questions
### Economic benefit
Who is economically exposed to the event, through what business/industry path, with what sensitivity, realization timing and financial consequences?

### Market selection / price formation
Why did attention, information diffusion, order flow or another mechanism select this stock/subtheme/market role at this moment rather than another economically related name?

A strong industrial beneficiary can be a weak short-term market leader. A sentiment/liquidity leader can have weaker direct earnings exposure. Do not collapse these roles.

## Investigation dimensions
These are hypotheses/dimensions, not a mandatory article outline.

### 1. Catalyst novelty / expectation gap
Ask whether the information is new, confirming, surprising, newly salient, probability-changing or timing-changing relative to prior expectations.

### 2. Concept / subtheme / economic-link map
Map materially relevant concepts, subthemes and A-share companies. Distinguish direct business/earnings exposure, economic linkage, second-order exposure, optionality and narrative-only association.

Vendor tags, media labels and concept membership are not proof of economic exposure.

### 3. Fundamental realization and sensitivity
Compare topic-specific channels such as price, volume, orders, capacity utilization, costs, revenue, margin, cash flow, capex, customer mix, exposure purity, earnings elasticity, order visibility, capacity headroom, valuation/expectation gap and realization timing.

### 4. Cross-sectional market selection
When explaining “why this one moved first/more”, test multiple mechanisms where evidence makes them relevant:
- information diffusion / lead-lag across economically linked firms or industries;
- investor attention, co-attention and narrative salience;
- order imbalance, liquidity, free float and price impact;
- prior positioning, crowding, expectation gap and available seller supply;
- market role such as fundamental beneficiary, sentiment leader, liquidity carrier or follower;
- price-limit mechanics, ranking salience and herding;
- market-cap / tradability / participant-structure differences;
- event-specific mechanisms supported by evidence.

Mechanisms can coexist or point in opposite directions. Price action proves that selection occurred; it does not identify the cause.

### 5. Continuation vs exhaustion / reversal
Do not infer persistence from a limit-up or strong move alone.

Persistence evidence may include repeated fundamental confirmation, continued information diffusion, new order/customer evidence, orderly broadening, persistent informed participation, limited supply response or another topic-specific mechanism.

Exhaustion/reversal evidence may include attention-only pressure, crowding, weak realization, widening narrative/fundamental gap, one-off order imbalance, liquidity distortion, failed follow-through or contradictory company evidence.

The article explains conditions; it does not turn them into entry/exit instructions.

## Causal-confidence ladder
Every material market-selection explanation uses one level:
1. `VERIFIED_FACT` — directly evidenced fact/relationship relevant to the claim.
2. `SUPPORTED_MECHANISM` — credible mechanism plus task-specific evidence, without proof it is the unique cause.
3. `PLAUSIBLE_INTERPRETATION` — reasonable interpretation with supporting signals but insufficient causal identification.
4. `UNRESOLVED` — competing explanations remain and evidence is insufficient.

Writing strength must match the level. Never turn `PLAUSIBLE_INTERPRETATION` into a statement such as “所以它涨停就是因为……”.

## Cross-sectional completeness when REQUIRED
The bounded research base should answer, where material:
- which companies/classes can benefit at all;
- which are direct, indirect, optionality or narrative-only;
- how benefit can reach revenue/profit/cash flow and on what horizon;
- why sensitivity differs;
- why market attention/price discovery may choose one earlier or more strongly;
- what evidence argues against the favored explanation;
- what can support continuation and what can imply exhaustion/reversal;
- which cases remain unresolved.

Completeness means covering every **material explanatory branch**, not asserting a confident answer for every stock.

## FINANCE_DEEP
When this module is active, FINANCE_DEEP must preserve every material branch needed to understand economic benefit, sensitivity differences, market selection, competing mechanisms, continuation/reversal and falsification boundaries. Low-materiality names may be grouped.

## FINANCE_BROAD
FINANCE_BROAD may compress the map around one to three high-value information gaps, but any market-selection claim it retains must keep the evidence grade and important counterevidence.

## Compliance boundary
This module may explain price formation, attention, liquidity, order imbalance, market roles, price-limit mechanics and continuation/reversal evidence. It must not produce target prices, timing, position sizing, stop levels or buy/sell/追涨/低吸 instructions.

## Durable priors
Research on attention, economically linked firms, information diffusion, liquidity/order imbalance and price-limit behavior may inform hypothesis generation. These are priors, not verdicts. Every article must verify current task-specific facts and can legitimately conclude `UNRESOLVED`.
