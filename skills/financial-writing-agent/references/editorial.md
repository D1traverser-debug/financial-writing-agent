# Editorial — Story Architecture Before Prose

Status: `CURRENT_CANDIDATE_1.0`
Owner: `DOMAIN:financial_writing`

## Purpose

Editorial converts an audited thesis/evidence base into a reader journey. It does **not** rewrite research, invent facts or prematurely write full publication prose.

The canonical current object is `financial_writing_agent.story_quality.StoryBlueprint`.

## Form follows content

Do not force every article into one universal structure. Before selecting the final route, compare genuinely different story engines when more than one is plausible. Current legal engine families include:
- hard anomaly;
- company conflict;
- reversal;
- mystery/question;
- system/value-chain map;
- consequence chain;
- character/case lead;
- timeline escalation.

Methods such as WSJ/nut-graf, five-box and hourglass are retained in the method knowledge catalog as candidate patterns. They are not mandatory shells.

## Story Blueprint requirements

A substantive long-form blueprint must define:
- reader job and starting state;
- article promise;
- central question;
- author stance/selectivity;
- chosen narrative engine and why it fits;
- concrete opening anchor;
- hard fact/scene/anomaly;
- the reader information gap;
- why the gap matters financially;
- early payoff and later payoff that remains worth reading for;
- explicit contiguous Chinese H1 sequence (`一、二、三、...`);
- section-level reader question;
- genuinely new information introduced by each section;
- named company/case anchors for material explanatory sections;
- value-chain class(es) covered;
- evidence refs;
- section tension/open loop;
- section payoff;
- bridge into the next section;
- ending payoff;
- voice evidence.

If a section cannot state what new information it adds, it is probably repetition or throat-clearing and should be removed/merged before drafting.

## Company-grounded explanation

When company evidence exists, material explanation should not spend pages in abstract industry exposition and bolt companies on later.

The default grounding obligation is:
`economic change -> named company/case -> observable number/behavior -> mechanism -> contrast/counterexample -> implication/boundary`.

This is **not** a fixed sentence order. The requirement is that the mechanism becomes observable through real companies/cases and evidence.

## FINANCE_DEEP

Deep is the complete finance/review product.

For thematic/industry work:
- every declared material value-chain class must appear in the blueprint unless explicitly adjudicated non-material to publication;
- material companies must receive enough coverage to distinguish economics, stage, sensitivity, evidence and adverse variables;
- company comparisons should name the companies and explain the current relative judgment/change conditions;
- all H1s use explicit Chinese numbering;
- there is **no default word-count ceiling**.

Control Deep by information density and progression, not arbitrary length. Remove redundancy and low-value context; do not remove material branches merely to fit a character target.

## FINANCE_BROAD

Broad is an independent acquisition rebuild from the same audited evidence, not a shortened Deep.

It may select only the strongest finance-wide information gaps, contradictions and company examples, but it still requires:
- its own StoryBlueprint;
- a real opening/payoff design;
- company/case grounding;
- numbered H1 structure;
- Publication Fitness review.

## Opening architecture

The opening should normally expose enough concrete material for a cold reader to orient, while leaving a material unresolved payoff.

Good openings can begin with:
- a hard number that conflicts with the obvious story;
- two companies moving differently under the same event;
- a concrete rule change with an unintuitive consequence;
- a case/scene that contains the larger mechanism;
- a reversal of a widely held assumption.

Do not waste the first paragraphs on generic importance statements, definitions the reader already knows or research-process narration.

## Section progression

Each H1 should answer one reader question and create a reason to continue. Useful progression patterns include:
- contradiction -> explanation -> second contradiction -> synthesis;
- upstream change -> company evidence -> downstream transmission -> boundary;
- case A -> case B counterexample -> mechanism -> value-chain map;
- policy event -> unit economics -> company divergence -> service/infrastructure spillover -> validation conditions.

The final article may use another pattern if the blueprint shows why it fits better.

## Ending

The ending must close the central question and leave the reader with a usable mental model, distinction, verification framework or change condition. Do not end with a generic “未来值得关注/持续观察” paragraph that merely repeats the article.

## Editorial admission

Current 1.0 tasks cannot leave `EDITORIAL` through the canonical current admission path without the required Deep/Broad StoryBlueprint(s). A story plan is therefore an executable prerequisite, not a paragraph in a style guide.
