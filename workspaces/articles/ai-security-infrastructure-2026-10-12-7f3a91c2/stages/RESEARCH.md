# RESEARCH — AI Security Infrastructure / 2026-10-12

## Working thesis
AI is not simply creating a new cybersecurity software subcategory. It is changing the traffic, workload, identity and release lifecycle of enterprise computing, causing part of cybersecurity spend to behave more like an unavoidable infrastructure tax. Overseas results already show this in hardware/network-security refresh plus platform ARR; China is only at an earlier realization stage, but several vendors now disclose orders or product revenue rather than only launches.

The stronger article angle is therefore **not** “cybersecurity sector recovery”. It is: **AI is creating a new security spending layer inside an otherwise still-differentiated cybersecurity industry.**

## Economic transmission chain
AI adoption → more east-west / machine traffic + denser high-value data-center workloads + new non-human identities/agents + new prompt/model/tool/supply-chain attack surfaces → security becomes required at network inspection, identity, runtime, model/app and SOC layers → spending moves from perimeter-only appliance purchase to a mixed stack of higher-throughput firewall, SASE/cloud security, AI application protection, agent identity/runtime control and AI-native security operations → vendors with installed-base leverage, performance advantages and productized AI-security offerings capture revenue/order growth earlier.

## Supported mechanisms
- **Infrastructure throughput mechanism:** AI data centers produce high east-west traffic and higher inspection throughput needs; this can accelerate replacement of legacy firewalls with higher-performance appliances/software.
- **Attack-surface expansion mechanism:** models/agents create new identities, tool calls, data-leakage and prompt-injection risks, adding security layers rather than eliminating existing ones.
- **Lifecycle shift mechanism:** security moves earlier from “after deployment monitoring” toward build-time assessment, pre-release testing, model/app firewalling and continuous runtime validation.
- **Platform-consolidation mechanism:** budget does not necessarily expand linearly; customers can reallocate toward integrated vendors that combine network, cloud, identity and SOC controls.

## Alternative / competing mechanisms
- Fortinet product strength may partly reflect an ordinary hardware refresh cycle and ASP mix, not only AI. Management itself cites both refresh/upgrade and AI workload security.
- Palo Alto’s reported growth includes acquisition effects; Q4 revenue/NGS ARR growth cannot be attributed solely to organic AI demand.
- CrowdStrike’s strength also reflects Falcon Flex adoption/platform consolidation, not an AI-security SKU alone.
- China AI-security revenue bases are still small and disclosure varies; triple-digit product growth can start from a low base.
- Traditional cybersecurity budgets in China remain constrained in some customer segments, so AI demand may create a new pocket of growth without lifting the whole sector.
