# Editorial module

## Principle

The research base may be broad; the article must be selective. Structure is derived from the topic's core contradiction, not from a fixed article template.

## TopicDNA

Before drafting, determine:
- what changed that is genuinely new;
- the main economic transmission chain;
- the most consequential reader misjudgment;
- one to three core contradictions;
- which company-comparison dimensions actually matter for this topic;
- what can be omitted or compressed without breaking the reasoning chain.

Generate multiple candidate structures **before** reading recent outline fingerprints. Use fingerprints only as a collision detector after the current topic has produced its own structures.

After TopicDNA, `references/structure_patterns.md` may be used as a design-pattern library to propose or combine candidate structures. It is not a rotation schedule or fixed article template.

## Information density

No default word-count target. Preserve: independent facts, key numbers, causal steps, counterexamples, why-this/why-not, market-selection mechanism, validation/falsification conditions and risk boundaries.

Compress first: repeated explanations, textbook background, synonym restatement, examples that do not change the judgment, and long company lists that add no comparison value.

## Two article modes

### FINANCE_DEEP
For finance communities. Preserve the full reasoning path and the competitive logic among mapped companies. Readers should be able to see how the conclusion was derived, not only the conclusion.

### FINANCE_BROAD
For broad-distribution platforms, but still a financial article. Translate a sector-specific event into a finance-wide question so that readers who mainly follow other sectors can still see why the article matters to investment judgment. Keep one complete reasoning chain; reduce entry cost, not analytical depth.

DUAL shares the same research base but independently edits title, opening, structure and emphasis for each mode.

## Hook engine

Hooks are a three-layer system:
1. **Headline:** earns the click.
2. **Opening / independent lead:** proves the click was worth it quickly.
3. **H1 headings:** create reasons to continue reading.

Headline generation is high-judgment. Generate 8-12 candidates from different logical angles, then judge and revise.

A strong headline typically makes clear:
- why this is finance-relevant;
- one primary contradiction or unresolved question;
- one credible factual anchor when available;
- a real information gap that the article will close;
- low enough cognitive load that the reader does not have to decode a list of parallel concepts.

Do **not** turn these into a fixed formula. A longer headline can have low cognitive load if it contains one simple conflict; a short headline can be overloaded if it stacks jargon.

For FINANCE_BROAD, ask: **why should a finance reader who mainly follows another sector still click this?** Use profit, valuation, pricing power, orders, cash flow, policy transmission, market selection, or another cross-sector investment question when supported by the research.

The headline must be fully兑现 by the opening. Do not use a mystery gap so large that the reader cannot identify the topic, and do not reveal the entire reasoning chain in the headline.

## Editorial judge

Judge title/opening/structure separately from the writer. Use PASS / REVISE / FAIL with explicit evidence. Revision feedback should identify the first point where the reasoning, information density or reader motivation breaks.
