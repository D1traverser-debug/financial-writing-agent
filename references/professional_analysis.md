# Professional financial-analysis integrity

## Purpose and ownership
This module owns **cross-layer analytical integrity**: whether a financial article moves from verified facts to industry effects, company economics, financial realization, valuation/expectations and market interpretation without silently skipping links or changing definitions.

It does not replace `research.md` source/coverage rules, `editorial.md` structure rules, `reader_value.md` reader-value rules or `platforms.md` distribution rules. It is not a mandatory fixed article outline. Apply only the analytical links material to the current thesis.

Professional depth is not the number of terms, companies or datapoints in the article. It is the ability to show which links are verified, which are inferred, which remain expected, which are already reflected in price, and what would break the conclusion.

## Evidence basis and jurisdiction boundary
This contract incorporates bounded analytical lessons from current professional/official sources:
- CFA Institute company-analysis and equity-valuation materials: company research connects business model, industry/competition, financial performance/position, forecasting, valuation and risks; assumptions should be clear and facts/opinions distinguished; analysis/forecast/valuation should be internally consistent and sufficiently transparent to critique.
- CFA Institute industry/competitive-analysis material: distinguish industry/macro-driven growth from company-specific share/competitive gains because the forecasting/valuation implications differ.
- China Securities Regulatory Commission 2023/2026 guidance on non-recurring gains/losses: assess economic substance, relationship to normal operations, industry/business model and materiality rather than mechanically treating labels as recurring/non-recurring.
- SEC MD&A and non-GAAP guidance is used **only as an analytical/comparability heuristic**, not as Chinese regulatory law: explain underlying causes/interrelationships rather than restating numbers, and do not compare custom financial measures across periods/companies without consistent definitions.
- Damodaran narrative/valuation material is used as an educational heuristic: a business narrative should map into economic/financial value drivers, while market price and intrinsic value are different concepts.

For A-share publication, Chinese law/accounting/disclosure rules and the issuer's actual reporting basis remain authoritative. Never import a foreign regulatory requirement as if it governed an A-share issuer.

## Analytical ladder: prove each material arrow
When the thesis spans several layers, reason through the applicable ladder:

`event / policy / technology / demand shock`
`→ industry variable`
`→ company exposure / competitive position`
`→ revenue / margin / earnings / cash-flow realization`
`→ valuation driver / expectation gap`
`→ market pricing / stock role`

Each arrow is a separate claim. Evidence supporting one layer does not automatically prove the next.

Common prohibited collapses:
- industry beneficiary ≠ every listed company is an economic beneficiary;
- business exposure ≠ material revenue/profit exposure;
- revenue growth ≠ profit growth;
- accounting profit ≠ cash generation;
- earnings growth ≠ earnings quality;
- good company ≠ attractive valuation;
- positive fundamental change ≠ positive expectation surprise;
- positive surprise ≠ guaranteed stock-price rise;
- market leadership ≠ strongest fundamental realization;
- signed framework agreement/order/capacity plan ≠ recognized revenue or cash flow.

When an intermediate link is unresolved, state the boundary and reduce the strength of the downstream conclusion.

## Industry beta vs company alpha
Do not attribute industry-wide effects to company-specific superiority without evidence.

When material, separate:
- market/industry growth;
- company volume growth versus price/mix effects;
- market-share change;
- customer/product/geography mix;
- capacity expansion and utilization;
- cost curve / gross-margin structure;
- competitive intensity and substitution;
- company-specific execution or one-off factors.

A company growing with the sector is analytically different from a company gaining share. A company gaining revenue through price inflation is different from one gaining physical volume. State which mechanism is actually supported.

## Financial metric definition contract
A material number should be interpretable, not merely precise-looking. For important financial/operating metrics, preserve enough definition to know what is being compared:
- reporting entity / segment where relevant;
- reporting period and whether point-in-time or period flow;
- unit/currency and material scale;
- actual, preliminary, forecast, guidance or analyst estimate;
- consolidated versus segment/company scope;
- gross versus net presentation where material;
- numerator/denominator for ratios;
- accounting-standard measure versus company-defined/adjusted operating metric;
- source/date and any material restatement or definition change.

Do not mechanically print all metadata in prose. The research/evidence layer must know it; the article exposes the parts needed to prevent a misleading comparison.

### Comparison discipline
Before saying a number accelerated, deteriorated, improved, exceeded or lagged, test comparability:
- YoY vs QoQ vs sequential/monthly comparisons are not interchangeable;
- low/high base effects can dominate percentage growth;
- TTM, latest quarter, FY actual and forward-year estimates are different periods;
- nominal and real growth differ when price/inflation matters;
- gross and net revenue/order values differ;
- consolidated and segment metrics differ;
- capacity, output, shipment, sales, order, backlog and recognized revenue are different concepts;
- a ratio comparison fails when numerator/denominator definitions change.

When definitions change between periods or peers, either normalize/reconcile them or state that direct comparison is not reliable.

## Recurring vs one-off and earnings quality
For A-share earnings analysis, do not treat reported net-profit growth as automatically equivalent to core operating improvement.

When material to the thesis, inspect the contribution and recurrence of items such as:
- non-recurring gains/losses and the company's disclosed classification;
- government grants/subsidies;
- asset disposals;
- fair-value/investment gains;
- impairment/reversal;
- tax effects;
- share-based payment or other unusual expenses;
- working-capital changes, receivables, inventory, contract liabilities and operating cash flow;
- capitalization/expense treatment or other accounting-estimate changes when material.

Use current PRC disclosure/accounting definitions. Do not call an item recurring/non-recurring merely because the label sounds familiar. Economic substance, business model, materiality and issuer disclosure matter.

A thesis that depends on earnings quality should explain why the profit is or is not likely to recur and whether cash/working-capital evidence supports the accounting result.

## Orders, capacity and realization stage
Keep realization stages separate:
- policy/technology narrative;
- qualification/customer entry;
- framework agreement / intention;
- signed order/contract;
- backlog;
- production/capacity readiness;
- shipment/delivery;
- revenue recognition;
- gross profit / operating profit;
- cash collection / operating cash flow.

Do not jump from a preceding stage to a later one without evidence. State order/backlog definitions and cancellation/fulfilment/recognition uncertainty when material. Capacity announcements are supply capability, not proof of utilization or demand.

## Forecast, scenario and sensitivity discipline
Forward-looking analysis should expose the drivers rather than hide them inside a point estimate.

When forecasts materially support the conclusion:
- identify the few assumptions that actually move revenue/margin/cash flow/valuation;
- distinguish company guidance, sell-side/market consensus, third-party forecast and the article's own inference;
- use scenario/range or sensitivity reasoning when uncertainty is high;
- do not add decimal precision unsupported by evidence quality;
- identify the first observable variable that would confirm or falsify the forecast.

A known trend is not automatically a forecast of its continuation. Explain what must remain true for extrapolation to hold.

## Valuation, expectation and price are different layers
Do not use “便宜/贵/低估/高估/空间大/估值修复” as decorative adjectives.

If valuation is material, state enough to understand the claim:
- valuation method or multiple;
- earnings/cash-flow period (for example TTM/FY1/FY2) and metric definition;
- own-history versus peer/industry comparison basis;
- key forecast assumptions;
- why the selected peers/benchmark are economically comparable;
- sensitivity to the drivers that matter.

Price and intrinsic/business value are not the same. A fundamentally positive event may already be priced in; a weak fundamental company may still be a short-term market leader. Keep these judgments separate.

### Expectation-gap discipline
“超预期/低于预期/预期差” requires a real expectation reference. Identify the referenced guidance/consensus/market expectation and its time boundary when available. If no credible expectation baseline exists, say that the result is strong/weak on an absolute or historical basis rather than inventing “超预期”.

Also separate:
- fundamental change;
- expectation change;
- catalyst that makes the change observable;
- current market pricing/positioning;
- short-term liquidity/sentiment response.

## Causality, alternatives and falsification
Correlation, chronology and causation are different.

For a material causal claim, ask:
- what mechanism connects X to Y;
- what intermediate observation should appear if the mechanism is true;
- what competing explanation could generate the same data;
- whether the evidence distinguishes those explanations;
- what observable fact would falsify the preferred explanation.

Do not pretend a counterfactual can be proven when it cannot. State causal confidence separately from factual confidence.

## Writing/application rules
Do not turn the analytical ladder into a repetitive six-section template. A short event article may only need two or three links; a company deep dive may need the full chain.

Use the minimum number of analytical layers needed to make the published conclusion defensible. Omit immaterial accounting/valuation machinery rather than displaying expertise for its own sake.

When a technical distinction changes the conclusion, explain its consequence in ordinary financial language. Jargon is useful only when it carries precision.

## Final-audit mapping
Do not create a duplicate final-evidence vocabulary. Prove professional analytical integrity through existing evidence keys:
- `GLOBAL.evidence_boundary`: definitions/source strength for material factual and numeric inputs;
- `GLOBAL.time_integrity`: actual/forecast/expectation baselines are time-consistent;
- `GLOBAL.counterexample_scan`: competing explanations and thesis-breaking evidence;
- `<MODE>.company_selection_basis`: industry-vs-company and realization-stage basis;
- `<MODE>.stock_judgment_clarity`: fundamentals, expectations, valuation and market role are not silently conflated;
- `<MODE>.reasoning_continuity`: each material arrow in the causal/economic chain is supported or explicitly bounded;
- `<MODE>.side_focus`: the chosen financial lens matches the current topic rather than forcing a generic valuation/earnings template;
- per-H1 `promise_payoff`: technical distinctions promised by the section are actually resolved.

A final article should revise rather than PASS when its headline/conclusion depends on a layer jump such as “行业景气→公司利润必增”, “利润增长→股价必涨”, or “高增长→估值便宜” without the missing analytical bridge.

## Failure patterns
Revise when any of these occur:
- percentage growth is quoted without noticing a base effect that changes interpretation;
- peer/company metrics have the same label but materially different definitions;
- preliminary forecast/guidance is written as audited actual data;
- a non-recurring item drives profit but prose presents it as core operating growth;
- orders/backlog/capacity are written as if they were recognized revenue;
- sector growth is presented as company share gain without evidence;
- revenue growth is presented as earnings/cash-flow improvement without margin/cash analysis when that link is material;
- “超预期” has no credible expectation baseline;
- “低估/便宜” has no valuation basis or mismatched forward/historical periods;
- a strong fundamental thesis is silently converted into a near-term price prediction;
- a short-term market leader is described as the strongest fundamental beneficiary merely because price action is strongest;
- a causal claim ignores an obvious competing explanation;
- technical detail is included only to sound professional and does not change the reader's judgment.
