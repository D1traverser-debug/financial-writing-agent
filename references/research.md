# Research module

## Goal

Research broadly enough that editorial compression never becomes research omission. The writer should know substantially more than the final article contains.

## Required reasoning coverage

- Establish the event, chronology, policy/industry/company facts and what actually changed.
- Map the relevant industry chain and the economically important transmission path.
- Build the relevant A-share universe for the article topic rather than starting from a few familiar names.
- For each material company, verify business relevance, business stage, customers/orders/revenue/profit/price/capacity when the topic requires them.
- Distinguish factual exposure from narrative exposure.
- Record negative evidence, counterexamples, stale facts and unresolved gaps.
- When the article discusses market selection, answer both `why_this` and `why_not`: why funds selected these names rather than other companies that also map to the catalyst.
- When needed, separate industrial beneficiary, earnings beneficiary, sentiment leader, liquidity carrier and other market roles instead of assuming they coincide.

## Evidence discipline

Maintain an evidence ledger with: claim, source, date, evidence type, confidence, and whether the claim is safe for title/H1/core conclusion use.

Facts used in the headline, opening, H1 or central conclusion require stronger verification than background facts. If evidence remains unresolved, downgrade or remove the claim; do not bridge the gap with confident prose.

## Scope

Research is global **within the current article topic**. It may use market-wide knowledge, but does not have to rerun an unrelated pre-market multi-event ranking tournament unless that competition is necessary to explain the article.

## Exit gate

RESEARCH passes only when the main thesis can be stated with an evidence chain, the relevant company universe has been considered, important counterevidence is represented, and unresolved gaps are explicitly bounded.
